PSA publishes stolen-card list after October shipment theft

PSA’s 23 October statement links a public certification list to the 13 October incident. Here is what that dated update establishes—and how to keep a useful paper trail.

Alex & Oscar BuyTrigger · Published 3 November 2026 · Source story 2026-10-23

What PSA reported

In its 23 October update, PSA said multiple customer orders were stolen from a FedEx truck following collection from its Santa Ana facility on 13 October. The company said local and federal investigators were involved.

PSA published a list covering 6,451 stolen items, including certification numbers and serial numbers where applicable. It said affected graded-card certificates had been deactivated and the list shared with marketplaces and auction houses. PSA also said it had refunded grading fees for affected orders, separately from the process for compensating the items themselves.

The statement described different arrangements depending on whether a customer used PSA’s return label, their own label or a dealer. Its compensation discussion refers to declared values and applicable terms; it is not a universal promise about every shipment. This article records that statement, not a fresh recovery announcement.

Collector context: make the evidence easy to find

A useful collection record should let you connect a physical card, its certificate, the purchase receipt and any shipping record without hunting through five apps. That is worth doing before a problem appears. Keep an original photograph of the slab and label alongside the order details; do not rely on a cropped social post as the only record of identity.

When checking a card connected to this incident, start with PSA’s original update and linked list. A concerning match calls for careful verification through the relevant official channel, not an accusation against a seller based on a screenshot. Equally, an attractive asking price is no reason to skip identity checks.

For our desk, the editorial distinction matters too: reporting a theft, explaining the company’s response and valuing an individual card are three different jobs. This story supplies no new market price and no assessment of a particular seller.
