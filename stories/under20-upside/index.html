Under $20. A $100 PSA 10. Is there actually a deal?

The price gap is a discovery filter. The copy, the evidence and the downside decide whether it survives.

Alex & Oscar BuyTrigger · Published 3 November 2026

The useful part of the idea

A raw card below US$20 with a PSA 10 reference around US$100 is easy to notice. That makes the gap useful for discovery. It is not enough to call the card undervalued: the two numbers describe different conditions, costs and risks.

The first job is to match the exact issue—set, collector number, language and variant. A cheaper card with similar artwork is not automatically the raw equivalent of the slab in a price chart.

Four checks before it becomes a target

  1. Can you actually enter? Use an available seller copy and its full delivered price, rather than a theoretical bargain that nobody is offering.
  2. Can you support the exit? Check recent exact-card sales in the same market and grade. Keep asks, guide estimates and completed sales distinct.
  3. What happens at PSA 9? Include the whole grading journey and selling charges. A PSA 10-only profit case can hide a substantial loss one grade lower.
  4. Does this copy justify more work? Review original front and back images, edges, corners and surface. Missing views remain missing evidence.

The $19 example fails the strong-profit test

In our calculator example, a US$19 entry and US$100 PSA 10 sale produce just US$16.01 after the assumed grading, other costs and selling charge. A 10% lower sale leaves US$7.51. The US$50 PSA 9 case loses US$26.49 before that stress.

That is a wide-looking gross price gap with a much smaller net outcome. It does not pass a demanding downside-and-return screen. Calling it a five-times opportunity would describe the price ratio, not the return on all money committed.

A shortlist can be empty

Targets should show why a candidate passes, what would invalidate it and when the evidence was checked. If no available copy meets the rules, the useful answer is no qualifying pick—not a longer list padded with attractive artwork.

This guide explains the research process. It does not recommend a current card, seller or grading submission.
