What TCG Modern Index is measuring—and what it isn't.

The basket, the grades, the missing observations and the difference between an index level and a card price.

Alex & Oscar BuyTrigger · Published 3 November 2026

A common starting point

An index puts the price paths of a defined basket onto a common scale. A baseline of 100 means the series starts at 100 points; it does not mean a card costs US$100, or that US$100 buys the basket.

For a simple two-card example with equal starting weights, one price rising 10% while the other falls 10% leaves the index at 100 before any rebalancing. The level tells you about the selected basket and method, not the whole hobby.

What is available in this edition

The Raw edition contains 100 exact English card issues, all with real catalogue artwork and historical Raw observations. The source date is 2 November 2026: 99 issues have a USD market guide; Empoleon has a separately labelled European reference because its current USD row is missing. The currencies are not combined.

Daily archives cover 1 January through 2 November. A card has no price before its release, and missing observations stay blank. PSA 9/10 numeric data, transaction counts and population statistics remain pending.

Raw, PSA 9 and PSA 10 stay separate

Different grades can trade differently and have different evidence coverage. Each series needs its own matched cards, dates and missing-data rules. A blank PSA 9 observation cannot be filled with a PSA 10 price. The three series are alternative views, never amounts to add together.

Provider guides are useful reference points, but they are not automatically independently verified completed sales. The page should show which kind of observation supports each figure.

Where this first edition stands

The original 90 issues remain in place and ten disclosed additions complete the public catalogue. Every card has some genuine Raw history, but no date has all 100 simultaneously priced. We cannot honestly publish a full-100 historical return from this source.

The full-year comparison therefore uses an explicit fixed basket of 82 issues with a USD guide for every day from 1 January through 2 November. Thirteen cards lack a January baseline; another five have source gaps: Latias & Latios GX, Rayquaza, Garchomp, Empoleon and Zoroark. The separate latest USD basket contains 99 issues and starts on 2 November, its first common valid date. It has one observation and no historical return yet.

Each basket starts with equal weights and fixed notional quantities. Prices then move the weights. All 100 cards remain in the catalogue with their individual available histories, and the private legacy series is unchanged.

Selection happened in November and earlier prices were reconstructed. Choosing for complete data can itself introduce bias. This is retrospective research, not a strategy selected in January. Top 50 remains a separate unfinished project.

What every change needs to explain

A new member needs an exact identity, a dated admission and a continuity calculation. Adding an expensive card must not manufacture a return. This first Raw version keeps its cohorts fixed; future membership or methodology changes require a new version.

Source-guide prices can jump when data is thin or the provider changes its estimate. These observations are preserved, not promoted as independently verified sales. The website is a dated snapshot; automatic daily updates are not active yet. TCG Modern Index is a research index, not shares in an investable fund.

Mew Blue’s 7 October archive row reports $5 before $1,702.50 on 8 October. That isolated source anomaly is held out of charts and returns; its original value and dated archive remain available in Evidence. No replacement price is invented.
